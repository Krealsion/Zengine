# A participant earns its place

**Decision record.** One decision, its alternatives, and why this one. Not a how-to — the law it
supports is in [participants](../workshop/participants.md).

**Context.** The split rule says to split a piece until it is too small to be useful alone, and
said nothing about how to tell. A census tapped every delivery of the whole test population and
the live Workshop (2.2 million deliveries) and measured each participant's partners, messages per
act and replacements. The snake's controls and clock were pipes: every use claimed for them -- a
pilot, a replay, a remap, a pace -- went through the world's own `SnakeTurn` and `SnakeTick`, and
folded into the world they cost fewer messages a tick and a key, and lost nothing exercised.

**Decision.** A piece earns its place when one of four holds, each measured: partners (two
product participants or more), an exercised replacement, an enforced authority or a held
resource, or a weaver's presence (a pane or tool owning state the weaver changes). With none, it
is a library of another. A pipe is judged by deletion; a mirror of another's derivation is a view
of data; a mixed piece is given the whole job.

**Alternatives considered.**
- *Every replaceable piece a weave* — tried: the snake's adapters were replaceable and no use
  replaced them; the census measured 3.27 messages a tick against 2.14 folded.
- *Permission as a fifth reason* — tried: the loader's `allow_any` admits every row but one, so
  it told no participant from another; only enforced authority counts.
- *Folding a mirror into the host* — argued: two owners would compute one fact; a view of data
  keeps the derivation with its owner.

**Consequences.** The snake's world keeps its own keys and beat, with its doors open by role and
its pace a field of its state, and a pilot or a replay switches the world's own off by a field
rather than unloading a weave: two drivers fight if nobody writes it.
