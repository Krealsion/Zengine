# A guest reads and points by door, and the weaver presses

**Decision record.** One decision, its alternatives, and why this one. Not a how-to — the law it
supports is in [guide](../workshop/guide.md).

**Context.** A Tutor on a weaver's desk reads where the weaver is and shows where to press next. A
guest's `capture` read every pane, the weaver's typing included, and no guest could point at
anything; a mark Workshop drew in its own voice would read as Workshop's guidance.

**Decision.** A row of the guests file's version 3 names the panes it reads (`capture`) and points
within (`guide`) as doors, compiled to Loom door rules Workshop asks about at each read and mark. A
pane outside them is its place, name and holder; what carries every pane is refused to such a row.
A mark names what a press names, a pane and an action id, is drawn by Workshop inside the pane's
body in a role of its own with the guest's name, and ends when the weaver presses it or its part
leaves. No guest reads Connections, the Hotkeys pane or the guests file, nor hears the weaver type
without `keystrokes`. The asker resolves a message to its controls from what it reads; Workshop
resolves nothing.

**Alternatives considered.**
- *Tried: a mark addressed by part, with a side declaration of causes* — the research's probe; it
  worked, and named a control two ways.
- *Argued: the read scope in Workshop's own table* — refused: a weaver's later grant could not
  widen it without a relaunch, and two stores would answer one question.
- *Argued: Workshop resolves a cause and marks it* — refused: a second site judging scope.

**Consequences.** `GuestRowDescribed`, the connections inventory and the surface roles each grew;
a by-pane reader hears no desk number and no `DeskStamps`, which name every pane, so it follows its
panes by reading them.
