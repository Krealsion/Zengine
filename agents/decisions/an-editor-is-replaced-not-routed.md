# An editor is replaced, not routed

**Decision record.** One decision, its alternatives, and why this one. The law it supports is in
[editor-switch](../workshop/editor-switch.md).

**Context.** A maker wants to exchange the Editor for a differently built one — the standard
Editor or a Neovim-backed one — while working, carrying the document, its unsaved edits, the
caret and the selection. Files, the Builder and Edit Code ask whoever holds `zengine.editor`, and
the desk routes the pane's keys to that office.

**Decision.** The implementations are authored as the load plan's `choices` for the office, and
a switch REPLACES the office's holder through Loom's prepared replacement: the destination is
loaded sealed, warmed with beats its coordinator relays, and given the document through an
authored handoff whose boundary is an ordinary message — the incumbent authors the exact
transfer there and holds still. The candidate adopts it as the transaction's preparation ask,
and the commit that moves the office is its activation. One native coordinator owns the
operation, and every ending answers the ask. Losses are consented to by a digest judged again at
the boundary. After the commitment nothing is rolled back: a failure is reported, and the
retired editor is kept.

**Alternatives considered.**
- *Both editors loaded, a router forwarding to the active one* — refused: every door would have
  to be taught the router, two custodians would hold one document, and a keystroke would have
  two subjects during the exchange.
- *The host naming the Neovim artifact* — refused: participation is authored by the plan only.
- *A reload of the office into another image* — refused: a reload carries one weave's own state
  shape, and two editors do not share one.
- *A timeout for a silent candidate* — refused: silence stays pending and cancellable, as an
  open's does.
- *Rolling back after the commitment* — refused: the successor holds the only current document.

**Consequences.** The standard Editor speaks the handoff as incumbent and as candidate; every
future editor speaks the same shapes. A sealed candidate receives no beat, so its coordinator
relays one. Workshop's Terminal is granted the switch's four asks, which is how a maker switches
today; a swapper pane and a confirmation modal are later presentations of the same office.
